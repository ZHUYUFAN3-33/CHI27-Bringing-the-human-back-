# Statistical Methods (draft)

> **TRIAL STORYLINE — draft text.** Numbers are taken from `numbers_sheet.md` and `tables.md` (generated by `paper_numbers.py`). The analysis plan described here was fixed before any inferential statistic was computed but was **not preregistered**; the manuscript must say so.

## Participants and analysis sample

Of the 316 people who opened the study on CloudResearch Connect, 300 completed it (1 was screened out for not being able to play video with sound, 15 left before finishing). The analysis sample comprises the 272 completers who passed both quality checks that were built into the questionnaire: an instructed-response attention item on the second clip page ("please select *Disagree*"; 21 failures) and a four-option comprehension question about the first clip (8 failures, one of them also failing the attention item). Cell sizes in the analysis sample ranged from 36 (AI only) to 41 (HA3). Participants were 18–78 years old (*M* = 41.2, *SD* = 13.8), 50% women, and resident in the United States (239), Canada (27), the United Kingdom (5) and Ireland (1); the median completion time was 15.3 min (IQR 12.7–19.7). Two sensitivity samples are reported: all 300 completers, and the analysis sample without the 15 participants who gave the same rating to every item on all three clip pages.

## Measures and scoring

After each clip, participants rated on 7-point agreement scales (1 = strongly disagree, 7 = strongly agree) three statements about OriHime — trustworthy for this task, useful for this task, and willingness to take part in such an interaction — which were averaged into an *OriHime evaluation* score (Cronbach's α = .79 across clips) and are also reported separately; one statement that the interaction *felt genuine* rather than like the execution of a program; and two statements about the controller named in the disclosure (*warm*, *competent*), plus, in the human conditions only, that the *operator was in control* of what OriHime said and did. After each clip participants also ranked the actors shown to them (human operator where present, AI system where present, OriHime or its provider, the person in the video) by responsibility for a described negative outcome and by credit for a described positive outcome. After the third clip, *performance expectancy* (4 items, α = .96) and *hedonic motivation* (3 items, α = .97) were rated about OriHime in general, followed by how much the participant *believed the description* of the operator (BEL1), and demographics. Before the disclosure, participants completed the Negative Attitudes towards Robots Scale (10 of the 14 items, three reverse-scored; α = .86), the short General Attitudes towards Artificial Intelligence Scale (positive subscale, 5 items, α = .92; negative subscale, 4 items reverse-scored, α = .85; reported separately as recommended by its authors) and Stereotype Content Model ratings of people with disabilities (competence, 5 items, α = .85; warmth, 4 items, α = .92). All pre-disclosure scales were administered on the same 7-point format as the outcome items.

## Analysis plan

The analysis sample, the four primary outcomes (OriHime evaluation, felt genuine, controller warm, controller competent), the primary model and three primary contrasts were fixed before any inferential statistic was computed. All other analyses are labelled *secondary*, *sensitivity*, *robustness* or *exploratory*. Analyses were conducted in Python 3.12 with statsmodels 0.14; code, per-test logs and the complete pairwise tables are provided as supplementary material.

## Primary model and contrasts

Clip-level outcomes were analysed with linear mixed-effects models with fixed effects of condition (seven cells), clip (relational, advice, collaboration) and presentation position (first, second, third), and a random intercept for participant, which accounts for the dependence among a participant's three ratings (intraclass correlations .40–.78). Models were fitted by restricted maximum likelihood; omnibus tests of factors and interactions used likelihood-ratio tests on maximum-likelihood fits; contrasts used Wald *z* tests. Contrasts were linear combinations of the seven cell means with equal cell weights: **C1**, the six human-involved cells versus the AI-only cell; **C2**, the three human-operator cells versus the three human-with-AI cells; **C3**, the four cells in which a disability was disclosed versus the two cells with no mention. **C4** (intellectual versus mobility disability) tests the pooling assumed by C3. Within the six human conditions, the control source × profile factorial was tested by likelihood-ratio tests, as were clip × condition and position × condition interactions. All 21 pairwise differences between conditions were also estimated for every outcome and are reported with unadjusted, Holm-adjusted and Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted *p* values within each outcome's family of 21 comparisons (Supplementary Table S1); the pairwise map was used to describe the pattern of differences, not to select contrasts. Participant-level outcomes (performance expectancy, hedonic motivation, belief) were analysed with ordinary least-squares models with the same contrasts.

Effect sizes are reported as the model estimate in scale points with a 95% confidence interval, and as Cohen's *d* computed on participant-level means (each participant's mean over the three clips) with the pooled standard deviation of the two groups compared. Primary contrasts are reported with unadjusted *p* and with Holm adjustment across the four primary outcomes; α = .05, two-sided, throughout.

## Assumption checks and robustness

Residuals were left-skewed (skewness −0.35 to −1.06) with ceiling effects (24–36% of ratings at the maximum). Levene's tests showed that between-participant variance was larger in the AI-only condition than in the human conditions (standard-deviation ratios 1.3–1.5; *p* ≤ .014), so the mixed model's single residual variance understates the uncertainty of contrasts involving that cell. Each primary contrast was therefore re-estimated under seven alternative specifications: the two sensitivity samples; covariate adjustment for centred pre-disclosure scores (GAAIS positive, NARS, SCM warmth) and age, which also addresses chance imbalance across cells in age and AI attitude; Welch's *t* test and the Mann–Whitney test on participant means; a participant cluster bootstrap (4,000 resamples of participants within cells, percentile intervals); and, for single-item outcomes, proportional-odds GEE models with participant clusters (global-odds-ratio dependence where it converged, otherwise an independence working correlation with cluster-robust standard errors). We describe a contrast as *robust* when it is significant under every specification and as *suggestive* when its significance depends on the equal-variance assumption.

## Equivalence tests

For the contrasts expected to be null under the storyline (C2, C3, C4) we report two one-sided tests (TOST) on participant-level means with Welch's correction, using equivalence bounds of ±0.35 *SD* — the smallest standardised difference detectable with 80% power for C2 in this sample — and ±0.50 *SD*. A contrast is called equivalent within a bound when both one-sided tests reject at α = .05, equivalently when the 90% confidence interval of *d* lies inside the bound.

## Attribution analyses

Whether an actor was ranked first was modelled with generalized estimating equations (binomial family, logit link, exchangeable working correlation, participant clusters) with clip as a covariate; effects are reported as odds ratios. Blame–credit asymmetry was tested at the participant level as the share of clips in which the actor was ranked first for responsibility minus the share for credit (Wilcoxon signed-rank test) and by GEE with question (responsibility versus credit) as a within-participant factor. Because conditions offered three or four actors, mean ranks are not comparable across conditions; full rankings were therefore summarised by Plackett–Luce worths (minorisation–maximisation estimation), which sum to one within each condition, with participant-bootstrap 95% intervals.

## Exploratory analyses

After the primary results were known, eight exploratory blocks (113 tests in total, all listed in Supplementary Table S2 with their *p* values) examined: moderation of the human-versus-AI contrast by pre-disclosure attitudes (GAAIS, NARS) and by belief in the description; the three components of the OriHime evaluation; moderation of the disclosure contrast by clip, by self-reported contact with people with disabilities and by stereotype content; equivalence tests; and follow-ups of two interaction hints. Interactions were tested by likelihood-ratio tests and conditional effects are reported at ±1 *SD* of the moderator. Belief in the description was measured after the disclosure and is therefore used only for sensitivity analysis, never for exclusion. These analyses are interpreted as hypothesis-generating; one of them (attitude moderation) had been noticed in an earlier descriptive pass of the data and is labelled post hoc.

## Missing data

No item was missing other than by design (the operator-control item and the operator/AI rows of the rankings, which were not shown in the conditions lacking that actor) and one participant's age, which excluded that participant from the covariate-adjusted model only.
