# Story D — In the eye of the sceptic

> Candidate storyline led by the exploratory attitude moderation (E1). **Lowest defensibility**: the headline finding is post hoc and needs replication; included to show what this frame would require.

## Title candidates
- In the Eye of the Sceptic: Prior Attitudes to AI Decide Whether an AI-Controlled Avatar Robot Feels Genuine
- Same Robot, Different Viewers: Attitude-Dependent Penalties for Disclosed AI Control

## Draft abstract (≈200 words)
Does describing an avatar robot as AI-controlled lower how its interactions are judged, or does that depend on who is watching? In an online experiment, 272 participants watched identical clips of the avatar robot OriHime after being told it was controlled by a human, by a human with AI assistance, or by an AI alone. On average, the AI-only description lowered perceived genuineness (d = .62) and warmth (d = .52). Exploratory moderation analyses showed that this penalty depended on participants' prior attitude to AI, measured before the disclosure: among participants one standard deviation below the mean attitude, the AI-only penalty was 0.4–0.9 scale points across outcomes (all p ≤ .015); among participants one standard deviation above, no outcome differed. The attitude slope for human-with-AI operation equalled that for human operation, so only the absence of a human triggered attitude dependence. We present this as a hypothesis-generating result and propose a preregistered replication.

## Contributions
1. A candidate moderator of disclosure effects (prior AI attitude) with a clear design for replication.
2. The average effects as context.

## Results order
1. 4.1 Checks. 2. 4.2 Average AI-only penalty [pre-specified]. 3. 4.3 Attitude moderation: interactions, conditional effects at ±1 SD, three-level slopes [exploratory, post hoc]. 4. 4.4 Bounded nulls (H vs HA, disclosure) [pre-specified]. 5. 4.5 Attribution [pre-specified]. 6. 4.6 Bookkeeping.

## Discussion themes
Heterogeneous reception of AI disclosure; implications for tailoring disclosure; a preregistered follow-up design (attitude measured, AI-only vs human, larger AI-only cell).

## Limitations that must lead
The headline is exploratory, seen in a descriptive pass before formal analysis, 3 of 14 moderation tests significant; the AI-only cell has 36 participants; +1 SD reversal not significant.

## Reviewer risks
- “An exploratory interaction cannot be the headline.” This is the decisive objection; CHI reviewers will raise it.
- “Attitude is confounded with the imbalance in A.” Adjusted models are needed and A remains small.

## What Study 2 must show
