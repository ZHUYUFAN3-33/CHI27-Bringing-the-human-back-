# Trial storyline — "Bringing the Human Back?"

> **STATUS: TRIAL.** This storyline was chosen on 2026-09-04 as a working frame for the Methods/Results drafts. It is not a decision. Every claim below is tagged with its evidence level; the tags must survive into the manuscript.

## One-sentence thesis

Telling people that a human is behind the robot changes how genuine, warm and useful the interaction feels — but only the *presence* of a human matters, not whether AI assists them and not who the human is; what AI involvement does change is who gets the blame and the credit.

## The arc

**Act 1 — the setup (Methods).** All participants watched the same three clips. The only thing that varied was one page of text: who controls OriHime (human / human with AI assistance / AI only) and, when a human was present, whether the operator was described as having an intellectual disability, a mobility disability, or not described at all. Any difference between conditions is therefore a difference in prior attribution, not in observed behaviour.

**Act 2 — what human presence changes (confirmatory, C1).** Compared with the six human-involved cells, the AI-only description made the interaction feel less genuine (d = .62, robust to every specification), the controller less warm (d = .52, suggestive: depends on the equal-variance assumption) and OriHime less useful and less worth taking part in (suggestive), while judgements of competence were unchanged. Trustworthiness itself did not differ. *Allowed:* "reduced perceived genuineness, warmth and usefulness." *Not allowed:* "reduced trust."

**Act 3 — what does not change (confirmatory equivalence, C2 and C3).** Human and human-with-AI operation were statistically equivalent on all perception measures (within ±0.35 SD for OriHime evaluation, genuineness, warmth and hedonic motivation; within ±0.50 SD for everything). Disclosing that the operator has an intellectual or mobility disability changed neither evaluations nor attributions (effects bounded within ±0.50 SD; no moderator found among clip, contact frequency, or stereotype content). The one measure that did respond to the AI-assistance text — perceived operator control — shows the text was read (d = .40). *Allowed:* "statistically equivalent within the stated bounds"; "no detectable effect, bounded." *Not allowed:* "identical"; "proves absence of bias."

**Act 4 — the cost lands on attribution (confirmatory, RQ4).** Adding AI assistance halved the odds that the human operator was held most responsible (72% → 55%, OR .47) or given most credit (71% → 51%, OR .42); the lost share went almost entirely to the AI (Plackett–Luce worth .28), not to the provider or the interlocutor. With no human present, the AI absorbed most of both (57–60%). In the human-with-AI condition the operator was slightly more likely to be blamed than credited (OR 1.21, small).

**Coda — who pays the AI-only penalty (exploratory, E1).** The penalty for "AI only" was concentrated among participants with less positive prior attitudes to AI (interaction with GAAIS: p = .019 for OriHime evaluation, .003 for competence; same direction on genuineness and warmth). Among AI-positive participants the two groups did not differ. The human-with-AI slope equalled the human slope: only the *absence* of a human triggers attitude-dependence. *Allowed:* "concentrated among AI-sceptical participants (exploratory)." *Not allowed:* "AI-positive participants preferred the AI-controlled robot."

## Stories the data cannot support

- "AI-controlled robots are trusted less" — trustworthiness did not differ (p = .10–.17 across methods).
- "Disclosing a disability changes how the operator is judged" — a bounded null; the two disability types did not differ either.
- "AI assistance undermines the human operator's standing" — perceptions were equivalent; only attribution shifted.
- "The disability effect appears in specific tasks or people" — 20 moderator tests, none significant.
- "People who like AI prefer the AI-controlled robot" — the reversed difference at +1 SD is not significant.
- "Position of the clip matters for competence" — the position × condition interaction decomposes into nothing.
